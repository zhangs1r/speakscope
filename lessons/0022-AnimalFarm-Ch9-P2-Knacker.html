Boxer's split hoof was a long time in healing. They had started the rebuilding of the windmill the day after the victory celebrations were ended. Boxer refused to take even a day off work, and made it a point of honour not to let it be seen that he was in pain. In the evenings he would admit privately to Clover that the hoof troubled him a great deal. Clover treated the hoof with poultices of herbs which she prepared by chewing them, and both she and Benjamin urged Boxer to work less hard. "A horse's lungs do not last for ever," she said to him. But Boxer would not listen. He had, he said, only one real ambition left—to see the windmill well under way before he reached the age for retirement.
Boxer 裂开的蹄子过了很久才好。胜利庆典一结束，他们就开始了风车的重建。Boxer 一天也不肯休息，并且把不让别人看出他在痛苦视为一种荣誉。晚上他会私下告诉 Clover 蹄子让他很疼。Clover 用自己嚼碎的草药给他做药膏外敷，她和 Benjamin 都劝 Boxer 少干点。"马的肺不可能永远撑得住，"她对他说。但 Boxer 不听。他说他只剩下一个真正的抱负了——在退休之前看到风车顺利运转起来。
poultices（药膏/敷剂）。Boxer 的"I will work harder"既是美德也是悲剧的根源。他至死都在为别人的目标耗尽自己。
At the beginning, when the laws of Animal Farm were first formulated, the retiring age had been fixed for horses and pigs at twelve, for cows at fourteen, for dogs at nine, for sheep at seven, and for hens and geese at five. Liberal old-age pensions had been agreed upon. As yet no animal had actually retired on pension, but of late the subject had been discussed more and more. Now that the small field beyond the orchard had been set aside for barley, it was rumoured that a corner of the large pasture was to be fenced off and turned into a grazing-ground for superannuated animals. For a horse, it was said, the pension would be five pounds of corn a day and, in winter, fifteen pounds of hay, with a carrot or possibly an apple on public holidays. Boxer's twelfth birthday was due in the late summer of the following year.
最初制定动物农场法律时，马的退休年龄定为十二岁，猪也是十二岁，牛十四岁，狗九岁，羊七岁，母鸡和鹅五岁。慷慨的养老金已经商定好了。但实际上还没有任何动物真正领过养老金退休，不过这个话题最近被讨论得越来越多。果园外的那块小田被留出来种大麦之后，有传言说大牧场的一角将被围起来，作为年老动物的放牧地。据说对一匹马而言，养老金将是每天五磅玉米，冬天十五磅干草，公共假日还有一根胡萝卜或者一个苹果。Boxer 的十二岁生日将在明年夏末到来。
superannuated（因年老而退休的）。注意 Orwell 的讽刺：革命初期承诺的"慷慨养老金"至今无一兑现——但动物们仍然在谈论和相信。Boxer 天真地憧憬着他的退休。
Meanwhile life was hard. The winter was as cold as the last one had been, and food was even shorter. Once again all rations were reduced, except those of the pigs and the dogs. A too rigid equality in rations, Squealer explained, would have been contrary to the principles of Animalism. In any case he had no difficulty in proving to the other animals that they were NOT in reality short of food, whatever the appearances might be. For the time being, certainly, it had been found necessary to make a readjustment of rations (Squealer always spoke of it as a "readjustment," never as a "reduction"), but in comparison with the days of Jones, the improvement was enormous. ... The animals believed every word of it.
与此同时，生活很艰苦。冬天和去年一样冷，食物更少了。除猪和狗之外，所有动物的口粮又一次被削减。Squealer 解释说，过分严格的口粮平等会违反动物主义的原则。无论如何，他毫不费力地向其他动物证明——不管表象如何——他们实际上并不短缺食物。当前确实有必要对配额进行"调整"（Squealer 总是说"调整"，从不说"削减"），但与琼斯时代相比，改善是巨大的。……动物们相信了每一个字。
readjustment——语言的操纵术：把"削减"重新命名为"调整"。这是 Orwell 在《政治与英语语言》中批判的核心现象：政治语言的目的就是让谎言听起来像真理。动物们愿意相信——"B 他们渴望相信生活比以前好。
There were many more mouths to feed now. In the autumn the four sows had all littered about simultaneously, producing thirty-one young pigs between them. The young pigs were piebald, and as Napoleon was the only boar on the farm, it was possible to guess at their parentage. It was announced that later, when bricks and timber had been purchased, a schoolroom would be built in the farmhouse garden. For the time being, the young pigs were given their instruction by Napoleon himself in the farmhouse kitchen. They took their exercise in the garden, and were discouraged from playing with the other young animals. About this time, too, it was laid down as a rule that when a pig and any other animal met on the path, the other animal must stand aside: and also that all pigs, of whatever degree, were to have the privilege of wearing green ribbons on their tails on Sundays.
现在需要喂养的嘴多了很多。秋天四头母猪几乎同时产仔，共生下三十一头小猪。小猪是花斑色的，而 Napoleon 是农场里唯一的公猪，所以它们的出身不难猜到。据宣布，以后一旦买到砖和木材，就会在农舍花园里建一间教室。目前这些小猪由 Napoleon 本人在农舍厨房里教授。它们在花园里活动，并被劝阻不要和其他小动物玩耍。也大约在这个时候，一条新规定被制定出来：猪和任何其他动物在路上相遇时，其他动物必须让路。此外，所有猪——不论等级——都享有周日尾巴上系绿丝带的特权。
littered（产仔）。piglets = Napoleon 的子女，正在被培养成新的特权阶层。"pig and any other animal"——语言的分类已经明示了"猪≠其他动物"。
The farm had had a fairly successful year, but was still short of money. ... Rations, reduced in December, were reduced again in February, and lanterns in the stalls were forbidden to save oil. But the pigs seemed comfortable enough, and in fact were putting on weight if anything. One afternoon in late February a warm, rich, appetising scent ... wafted itself across the yard from the little brew-house ... Someone said it was the smell of cooking barley. The animals sniffed the air hungrily and wondered whether a warm mash was being prepared for their supper. But no warm mash appeared, and on the following Sunday it was announced that from now onwards all barley would be reserved for the pigs. ... every pig was now receiving a ration of a pint of beer daily, with half a gallon for Napoleon himself, which was always served to him in the Crown Derby soup tureen.
农场度过了相当成功的一年，但仍然缺钱。……十二月削减的口粮二月又削减了一次，马厩里的灯笼也被禁止使用以节省灯油。但猪们看起来相当舒适，实际上还在增肥。二月下旬的一个下午，一股温暖浓郁的香味从闲置已久的小酿酒间飘过院子……有动物说是煮大麦的味道。动物们饥饿地嗅着空气，想知道是不是在给他们准备热乎乎的晚饭。但没有热食出现，接下来的周日宣布——从现在起所有大麦都留给猪吃。……每头猪每天都能分到一品脱啤酒，Napoleon 自己则有半加仑，总是用 Crown Derby 汤碗盛上来。
tureen（大而深的盖碗）。Crown Derby 是英国名贵瓷器。动物在挨饿，猪在用高级瓷器喝啤酒。Orwell 用"putting on weight"与粮食短缺的对比强化阶级分化的讽刺。
But if there were hardships to be borne, they were partly offset by the fact that life nowadays had a greater dignity than it had had before. There were more songs, more speeches, more processions. Napoleon had commanded that once a week there should be held something called a Spontaneous Demonstration, the object of which was to celebrate the struggles and triumphs of Animal Farm. ... The sheep were the greatest devotees of the Spontaneous Demonstration, and if anyone complained (as a few animals sometimes did, when no pigs or dogs were near) that they wasted time and meant a lot of standing about in the cold, the sheep were sure to silence him with a tremendous bleating of "Four legs good, two legs bad!" ... So that, what with the songs, the processions, Squealer's lists of figures, the thunder of the gun, the crowing of the cockerel, and the fluttering of the flag, they were able to forget that their bellies were empty, at least part of the time.
但如果说有苦要受，它们被生活如今比从前更有尊严这一事实部分抵消了。歌声更多了，演讲更多了，游行更多了。Napoleon 下令每周应举行一次所谓的"自发表演"，目的是庆祝动物农场的斗争和胜利。……羊是自发表演最狂热的拥趸，如果有谁（偶尔有几只动物）抱怨这浪费了时间，让他们在寒风里站了太久——只要没有猪和狗在旁边——羊群就会被用排山倒海的"四条腿好，两条腿坏！"将其淹没。……于是，靠着歌声、游行、Squealer的数据清单、炮声、公鸡的啼鸣和旗帜的飘扬，他们至少能在一部分时间里忘记自己空荡荡的肚子。
Spontaneous Demonstration——"自发"却由最高领导人"命令"举行，这是 Orwell 对极权宣传语言最辛辣的讽刺之一。羊群扮演了压制异议的角色，与现实中的舆论机器如出一辙。
In April, Animal Farm was proclaimed a Republic, and it became necessary to elect a President. There was only one candidate, Napoleon, who was elected unanimously. On the same day it was given out that fresh documents had been discovered which revealed further details about Snowball's complicity with Jones. It now appeared that Snowball had not, as the animals had previously imagined, merely attempted to lose the Battle of the Cowshed by means of a stratagem, but had been openly fighting on Jones's side. ... The wounds on Snowball's back, which a few of the animals still remembered to have seen, had been inflicted by Napoleon's teeth.
四月，动物农场被宣布为共和国，需要选举一位总统。只有一位候选人——Napoleon——全票当选。同一天，据称发现了新文件，揭示了 Snowball 与 Jones 勾结的更多细节。现在看起来 Snowball 并不像动物们之前想象的那样只是在牛棚战役中试图用计谋失败——他实际上是公开站在 Jones 一方作战的。……Snowball 背上的一些动物还记得看到过的伤疤，是 Napoleon 的牙咬出来的。
complicity（共谋/勾结）。历史被不断改写——Snowball 从英雄变成叛徒。一年前还是 Napoleon 亲自咬伤了 Snowball 并把他赶出农场，现在却成了 Snowball 站在人类那边作战的证据。
In the middle of the summer Moses the raven suddenly reappeared on the farm, after an absence of several years. He was quite unchanged, still did no work, and talked in the same strain as ever about Sugarcandy Mountain. He would perch on a stump, flap his black wings, and talk by the hour to anyone who would listen. "Up there, comrades," he would say solemnly, pointing to the sky with his large beak—"up there, just on the other side of that dark cloud that you can see—there it lies, Sugarcandy Mountain, that happy country where we poor animals shall rest for ever from our labours!" ... A thing that was difficult to determine was the attitude of the pigs towards Moses. They all declared contemptuously that his stories about Sugarcandy Mountain were lies, and yet they allowed him to remain on the farm, not working, with an allowance of a gill of beer a day.
仲夏，渡鸦 Moses 在消失了几年后突然重新出现在农场。他完全没变，仍然不干活，一如既往地大谈"糖果山"。"在那里，同志们，"他用大喙指着天空庄严地说，"就在你能看到的那朵乌云的另一边——糖果山就在那里，那个快乐的地方，我们可怜的动物将永远从劳动中安息！"……很难判断猪对 Moses 的态度。他们都轻蔑地宣称他的糖果山故事是谎言，但他们允许他留在农场，不干活，每天还能领到一杯啤酒。
Moses 和 Sugarcandy Mountain 代表宗教。 猪们不信宗教但保留它——因为宗教可以安抚底层（"这辈子受苦，死后上天堂"）。Moses 不干活喝啤酒，就是有组织的宗教的缩影。
After his hoof had healed up, Boxer worked harder than ever. Indeed, all the animals worked like slaves that year. Apart from the regular work of the farm, and the rebuilding of the windmill, there was the schoolhouse for the young pigs, which was started in March. Sometimes the long hours on insufficient food were hard to bear, but Boxer never faltered. In nothing that he said or did was there any sign that his strength was not what it had been. It was only his appearance that was a little altered; his hide was less shiny than it had used to be, and his great haunches seemed to have shrunken. The others said, "Boxer will pick up when the spring grass comes on"; but the spring came and Boxer grew no fatter. Sometimes on the slope leading to the top of the quarry, when he braced his muscles against the weight of some vast boulder, it seemed that nothing kept him on his feet except the will to continue. At such times his lips were seen to form the words, "I will work harder"; he had no voice left. Once again Clover and Benjamin warned him to take care of his health, but Boxer paid no attention. His twelfth birthday was approaching.
Boxer 的蹄子愈合后，干得比以前更拼命了。事实上，那一年所有动物都像奴隶一样工作。除了农场的常规工作和风车重建，还有三月份开始搭建的幼猪校舍。长时间吃不饱地干着活，有时确实难以忍受，但 Boxer 从不退缩。从一言一行中都看不出他的力量已不如从前。只有外表略有变化——皮毛没以前亮了，巨大的臀部似乎萎缩了。其他动物说："等春天的草长起来 Boxer 就会恢复的。"但春天来了，Boxer 没有变胖。有时在通往采石场顶端的坡上，当他绷紧肌肉对抗巨石的重压时，似乎支撑他站着的只有继续下去的意志。这时他的嘴形会动着，说出"I will work harder"——他已经没有声音了。Clover 和 Benjamin 再次警告他注意身体，但 Boxer 不予理会。他的十二岁生日快到了。
faltered（犹豫/动摇）。"I will work harder"——Boxer 至死捍卫的信念。注意"nothing kept him on his feet except the will to continue"——这既是英雄主义的最高表达，也是悲剧的预言。
Late one evening in the summer, a sudden rumour ran round the farm that something had happened to Boxer. He had gone out alone to drag a load of stone down to the windmill. And sure enough, the rumour was true. A few minutes later two pigeons came racing in with the news; "Boxer has fallen! He is lying on his side and can't get up!"
夏夜，农场突然传开了一个谣言——Boxer 出事了。他独自出去拉一车石头到风车那里。谣言确实是真的。几分钟后两只鸽子疾飞而来报告消息："Boxer 摔倒了！他侧躺在地上，站不起来了！"
About half the animals on the farm rushed out to the knoll where the windmill stood. There lay Boxer, between the shafts of the cart, his neck stretched out, unable even to raise his head. His eyes were glazed, his sides matted with sweat. A thin stream of blood had trickled out of his mouth. Clover dropped to her knees at his side.
农场里大约一半的动物冲到了风车所在的小山丘。Boxer 躺在那儿，夹在车辕之间，脖子伸得长长的，连头都抬不起来。他的眼睛呆滞，两肋沾满了汗。一缕细血从他嘴角流出来。Clover 跪在了他身边。
"Boxer!" she cried, "how are you?"
"Boxer！"她喊道，"你怎么样？"
"It is my lung," said Boxer in a weak voice. "It does not matter. I think you will be able to finish the windmill without me. There is a pretty good store of stone accumulated. I had only another month to go in any case. To tell you the truth, I had been looking forward to my retirement. And perhaps, as Benjamin is growing old too, they will let him retire at the same time and be a companion to me."
"是我的肺，"Boxer 用虚弱的声音说。"没关系。我觉得没有我你们也能把风车建好。已经积攒了相当多的石头了。我无论如何只剩一个月了。说实话，我一直期待着退休。也许，因为 Benjamin 也老了，他们会让他同时退休，做我的伙伴。"
这是全书最令人心碎的段落之一。Boxer 到最后一刻仍然在为别人着想——希望 Benjamin 也能退休陪他。他完全不知道等待他的是什么。
"We must get help at once," said Clover. "Run, somebody, and tell Squealer what has happened."
"我们必须马上找人帮忙，"Clover 说，"快，谁去告诉 Squealer 发生了什么。"
All the other animals immediately raced back to the farmhouse to give Squealer the news. Only Clover remained, and Benjamin who lay down at Boxer's side, and, without speaking, kept the flies off him with his long tail. After about a quarter of an hour Squealer appeared, full of sympathy and concern. He said that Comrade Napoleon had learned with the very deepest distress of this misfortune to one of the most loyal workers on the farm, and was already making arrangements to send Boxer to be treated in the hospital at Willingdon. The animals felt a little uneasy at this. Except for Mollie and Snowball, no other animal had ever left the farm, and they did not like to think of their sick comrade in the hands of human beings. However, Squealer easily convinced them that the veterinary surgeon in Willingdon could treat Boxer's case more satisfactorily. And about half an hour later, when Boxer had somewhat recovered, he was with difficulty got on to his feet, and managed to limp back to his stall, where Clover and Benjamin had prepared a good bed of straw for him.
其他所有动物立刻跑回农舍向 Squealer 报告消息。只有 Clover 留下了，还有 Benjamin——他躺在 Boxer 身边，一言不发，用长尾巴驱赶苍蝇。大约一刻钟后 Squealer 出现了，满怀同情和关切。他说 Napoleon 同志以最深切的悲痛得知了农场最忠诚劳动者之一的不幸，已经在安排送 Boxer 去 Willingdon 的医院治疗。动物们对此感到有些不安。除了 Mollie 和 Snowball，没有动物离开过农场，他们不愿意想到生病的同志落入人类手中。但 Squealer 轻易地说服了他们——Willingdon 的兽医能比在农场更好地治疗 Boxer。半小时后 Boxer 稍微恢复了些，被艰难地扶起来，一瘸一拐回到自己的马厩，Clover 和 Benjamin 已经为他铺好了舒适的草床。
For the next two days Boxer remained in his stall. The pigs had sent out a large bottle of pink medicine which they had found in the medicine chest in the bathroom, and Clover administered it to Boxer twice a day after meals. In the evenings she lay in his stall and talked to him, while Benjamin kept the flies off him. Boxer professed not to be sorry for what had happened. If he made a good recovery, he might expect to live another three years, and he looked forward to the peaceful days that he would spend in the corner of the big pasture. It would be the first time that he had had leisure to study and improve his mind. He intended, he said, to devote the rest of his life to learning the remaining twenty-two letters of the alphabet.
接下来的两天 Boxer 待在马厩里。猪们送来了一大瓶粉色药水——他们在浴室药箱里找到的——Clover 每天饭后给 Boxer 喂两次。晚上她躺在马厩里和他聊天，Benjamin 则帮他驱赶苍蝇。Boxer 说他对发生的事并不感到遗憾。如果能好好恢复，他还能再活三年，他期待着在大牧场角落里度过的平静日子。那将是他第一次有空闲学习和提升自己。他说他打算用余生来学习字母表中剩下的二十二个字母。
Boxer 只学会了字母表的前四个字母——他一辈子都在工作，从未有过学习的闲暇。他对退休后"学习二十二个字母"的憧憬，像孩子一样天真，却永远不会实现。
However, Benjamin and Clover could only be with Boxer after working hours, and it was in the middle of the day when the van came to take him away. The animals were all at work weeding turnips under the supervision of a pig, when they were astonished to see Benjamin come galloping from the direction of the farm buildings, braying at the top of his voice. It was the first time that they had ever seen Benjamin excited—indeed, it was the first time that anyone had ever seen him gallop. "Quick, quick!" he shouted. "Come at once! They're taking Boxer away!" Without waiting for orders from the pig, the animals broke off work and raced back to the farm buildings. Sure enough, there in the yard was a large closed van, drawn by two horses, with lettering on its side and a sly-looking man in a low-crowned bowler hat sitting on the driver's seat. And Boxer's stall was empty.
然而，Benjamin 和 Clover 只能在劳作之余陪伴 Boxer。货车来带他走的时候，是正午。动物们正在一头猪的监督下在萝卜地里除草，他们惊讶地看到 Benjamin 从农舍方向狂奔而来，声嘶力竭地大叫。这是他们第一次看到 Benjamin 激动——事实上，这是任何人第一次看到他飞奔。"快！快！"他喊道，"快来！他们要把 Boxer 带走了！"没等猪下命令，动物们就扔下活计冲回了农舍。果然，院子里停着一辆大型封闭货车，由两匹马拉着，车身侧面有字，驾驶座上坐着一个戴着矮圆顶礼帽、眼神狡诈的男人。Boxer 的马厩空了。
braying（驴叫）。gallop（飞奔）。Benjamin 一辈子沉默犬儒、从不动容——这是全书唯一一次他失控狂奔。当最犬儒的人开始行动时，意味着事情已经彻底无法挽回了。
The animals crowded round the van. "Good-bye, Boxer!" they chorused, "good-bye!"
动物们围在货车旁。"再见，Boxer！"他们齐声喊道，"再见！"
"Fools! Fools!" shouted Benjamin, prancing round them and stamping the earth with his small hoofs. "Fools! Do you not see what is written on the side of that van?"
"傻瓜！傻瓜！"Benjamin 喊道，围着他们跳着，用小蹄子跺着地面。"傻瓜！你们没看到车身上写的什么吗？"
That gave the animals pause, and there was a hush. Muriel began to spell out the words. But Benjamin pushed her aside and in the midst of a deadly silence he read:
动物们愣住了，一片寂静。Muriel 开始拼读那些字。但 Benjamin 把她推开，在死一般的寂静中念道：
"'Alfred Simmonds, Horse Slaughterer and Glue Boiler, Willingdon. Dealer in Hides and Bone-Meal. Kennels Supplied.' Do you not understand what that means? They are taking Boxer to the knacker's!"
"'阿尔弗雷德·西蒙兹，Willingdon 马匹屠宰与熬胶商。皮张与骨粉经销商。狗舍供应。'你们不明白这意味着什么吗？他们要把 Boxer 送到屠马场去！"
slaughterer（屠宰商），knacker（收购废马并加工成动物饲料/胶水的人）。这是全书最令人震惊的时刻之一。Orwell 用一整串骇人的商业名称来击碎读者的期待——这些名词越具体越令人不寒而栗。
A cry of horror burst from all the animals. At this moment the man on the box whipped up his horses and the van moved out of the yard at a smart trot. All the animals followed, crying out at the tops of their voices. Clover forced her way to the front. The van began to gather speed. Clover tried to stir her stout limbs to a gallop, and achieved a canter. "Boxer!" she cried. "Boxer! Boxer! Boxer!" And just at this moment, as though he had heard the uproar outside, Boxer's face, with the white stripe down his nose, appeared at the small window at the back of the van.
所有动物爆发出恐怖的叫喊。这时驾驶座上的男人鞭打马匹，货车以迅疾的小跑驶出了院子。所有动物跟在后面，声嘶力竭地喊着。Clover 挤到了最前面。货车开始加速。Clover 试图让她笨重的四肢飞奔起来，但只能做到慢跑。"Boxer！"她喊道。"Boxer！Boxer！Boxer！"就在这时，仿佛听到了外面的喧闹，Boxer 那张鼻子上带着白色条纹的脸出现在货车后面的小窗上。
trot（小跑），canter（慢跑）。Clover 连 gallop 都做不到了——她老了。Boxer 的脸出现在窗口那一幕让人联想到囚车。
"Boxer!" cried Clover in a terrible voice. "Boxer! Get out! Get out quickly! They're taking you to your death!"
"Boxer！"Clover 用可怕的声音喊道，"Boxer！出来！快出来！他们要把你送去死！"
All the animals took up the cry of "Get out, Boxer, get out!" But the van was already gathering speed and drawing away from them. It was uncertain whether Boxer had understood what Clover had said. But a moment later his face disappeared from the window and there was the sound of a tremendous drumming of hoofs inside the van. He was trying to kick his way out. The time had been when a few kicks from Boxer's hoofs would have smashed the van to matchwood. But alas! his strength had left him; and in a few moments the sound of drumming hoofs grew fainter and died away. In desperation the animals began appealing to the two horses which drew the van to stop. "Comrades, comrades!" they shouted. "Don't take your own brother to his death!" But the stupid brutes, too ignorant to realise what was happening, merely set back their ears and quickened their pace. Boxer's face did not reappear at the window. Too late, someone thought of racing ahead and shutting the five-barred gate; but in another moment the van was through it and rapidly disappearing down the road. Boxer was never seen again.
所有动物都跟着喊"出来，Boxer，出来！"但货车已经在加速离他们远去。不确定 Boxer 是否听懂了 Clover 的话。但片刻之后他的脸从窗口消失了，货车里传来巨大的蹄子敲击声——他正试图踢开一条路。曾经的 Boxer 只要踢几脚就能把货车踢成碎片。但可惜！他的力量已经离他而去；几秒钟后踢蹄声越来越弱直至消失。绝望之中动物们开始向拉车的两匹马喊话："同志们，同志们！不要把你们的兄弟送去死！"但那两匹愚蠢的牲口，太无知了，完全没意识到发生了什么，只是竖起耳朵加快了步伐。Boxer 的脸没有再出现在窗口。太晚了——有人想到跑到前面关上那道五栏门——但货车已经穿了过去，迅速在路上消失了。Boxer 再也没有被见到过。
alas!（唉！）。这是 Orwell 罕见的直接抒情——他平时极为克制。"The time had been when... But alas!"——曾经能踢碎货车的马蹄如今连站都站不稳了。拉车的两匹马不认识 Boxer，无意中参与了谋杀——这是极权体制下普通人的共谋隐喻。
Three days later it was announced that he had died in the hospital at Willingdon, in spite of receiving every attention a horse could have. Squealer came to announce the news to the others. He had, he said, been present during Boxer's last hours.
三天后宣布——尽管接受了马能得到的一切照顾——Boxer 在 Willingdon 医院去世了。Squealer 来向大家宣布这个消息。他说，Boxer 临终时他就在场。
"It was the most affecting sight I have ever seen!" said Squealer, lifting his trotter and wiping away a tear. "I was at his bedside at the very last. And at the end, almost too weak to speak, he whispered in my ear that his sole sorrow was to have passed on before the windmill was finished. 'Forward, comrades!' he whispered. 'Forward in the name of the Rebellion. Long live Animal Farm! Long live Comrade Napoleon! Napoleon is always right.' Those were his very last words, comrades."
"这是我见过的最感人的一幕！"Squealer 抬起猪蹄擦去一滴眼泪。"我就在他的床边，直到最后一刻。临终时，他虚弱得几乎说不出话，他对我耳语说他唯一的遗憾是在风车完工前就离开了。'前进，同志们！'他低声说，'以起义的名义前进。动物农场万岁！Napoleon 同志万岁！Napoleon 永远正确。'这就是他的临终遗言，同志们。"
trotter（猪蹄）。Squealer 编造的"遗言"里 Boxer 说的全是政治口号——"Napoleon is always right"——而实际上 Boxer 生前最后的愿望是退休后和 Benjamin 一起学习字母。宣传机器把个体的人性彻底抹杀了。
Here Squealer's demeanour suddenly changed. He fell silent for a moment, and his little eyes darted suspicious glances from side to side before he proceeded.
这时 Squealer 的表情突然变了。他沉默了片刻，小眼睛警惕地向两边扫视了一番，才继续往下说。
It had come to his knowledge, he said, that a foolish and wicked rumour had been circulated at the time of Boxer's removal. Some of the animals had noticed that the van which took Boxer away was marked "Horse Slaughterer," and had actually jumped to the conclusion that Boxer was being sent to the knacker's. It was almost unbelievable, said Squealer, that any animal could be so stupid. ... But the explanation was really very simple. The van had previously been the property of the knacker, and had been bought by the veterinary surgeon, who had not yet painted the old name out. That was how the mistake had arisen.
他说，他得知在 Boxer 被带走时流传了一个愚蠢而恶毒的谣言。有些动物注意到带走 Boxer 的车上写着"马匹屠宰商"，就断定 Boxer 被送去了屠马场。Squealer 说，任何动物会愚蠢到这种程度几乎令人难以置信。……但解释其实很简单。那辆车以前是屠马场的财产，被兽医买下后还没来得及把旧名字刷掉。误会就是这样产生的。
Squealer 的谎言既无耻又高效。他用"stupid"来攻击目睹真相的动物——这是 gaslighting（煤气灯效应）的经典操作：让受害者怀疑自己的判断。注意他"darted suspicious glances"——他知道自己在撒谎。
The animals were enormously relieved to hear this. And when Squealer went on to give further graphic details of Boxer's death-bed, the admirable care he had received, and the expensive medicines for which Napoleon had paid without a thought as to the cost, their last doubts disappeared and the sorrow that they felt for their comrade's death was tempered by the thought that at least he had died happy.
动物们听到这里大大松了口气。当 Squealer 继续绘声绘色地描述 Boxer 临终细节、他得到的无微不至的照顾、以及 Napoleon 不计代价为他支付的昂贵药费时，他们最后的疑虑也消失了——失去同志的悲伤被"至少他死得很安详"的想法冲淡了。
"at least he had died happy"——这是全书最残酷的句子之一。Boxer 被欺骗、被剥削、被杀害，而他的朋友们相信他是幸福的。自我欺骗是极权能够持久运转的燃料。
Napoleon himself appeared at the meeting on the following Sunday morning and pronounced a short oration in Boxer's honour. It had not been possible, he said, to bring back their lamented comrade's remains for interment on the farm, but he had ordered a large wreath to be made from the laurels in the farmhouse garden and sent down to be placed on Boxer's grave. And in a few days' time the pigs intended to hold a memorial banquet in Boxer's honour. Napoleon ended his speech with a reminder of Boxer's two favourite maxims, "I will work harder" and "Comrade Napoleon is always right"—maxims, he said, which every animal would do well to adopt as his own.
第二天周日上午 Napoleon 亲自出现在集会上，发表了简短的悼词。他说，无法把令人怀念的同志的遗体带回农场安葬，但他订购了一个大花圈——用农舍花园里的月桂做成的——送到 Boxer 的墓前。几天后猪们打算为 Boxer 举办一个纪念宴会。Napoleon 在演讲结束时提醒大家 Boxer 的两句最爱格言："我会更努力地工作"和"Napoleon 同志永远正确"——他说，这些格言每个动物都应该采纳为自己的准则。
interment（安葬）。Napoleon 把 Boxer 的口号拿来当政治工具——尤其是"Comrade Napoleon is always right"——这根本不是 Boxer 的原话（那是 Squealer 编造的"临终遗言"里的）。而 Boxer 真正说过的关于退休和学习的愿望，一字不提。
On the day appointed for the banquet, a grocer's van drove up from Willingdon and delivered a large wooden crate at the farmhouse. That night there was the sound of uproarious singing, which was followed by what sounded like a violent quarrel and ended at about eleven o'clock with a tremendous crash of glass. No one stirred in the farmhouse before noon on the following day, and the word went round that from somewhere or other the pigs had acquired the money to buy themselves another case of whisky.
宴会当天，一辆杂货店货车从 Willingdon 开来，在农舍卸下了一个大木箱。当晚传来喧闹的歌声，接着是一场激烈的争吵，大约十一点钟伴随着巨大玻璃破碎声结束。直到第二天中午之前农舍里都没有任何动静，消息传开——猪们不知从哪儿搞到了钱，给自己又买了一箱威士忌。
本章以 Boxer 一生的悲剧开始，以猪们在"纪念宴会"上喝醉砸玻璃结束。真正的讽刺：被纪念者 Boxer 被谋杀，纪念者毫不愧疚地大吃大喝。Orwell 没有写任何动物提出质疑。
章节总结

Ch.IX 是全书最令人心碎的一章。Boxer——最忠诚、最善良、最强大的劳动者——被欺骗、耗尽、最终杀害。

Orwell 在这一章中展示了极权统治的完善：
• 语言的操纵（"readjustment"取代"reduction"）
• 历史的重写（Snowball 从英雄变成叛徒）
• 宗教的利用（允许 Moses 传播糖果山）
• 宣传的谎言（Squealer 编造的临终遗言）
• 粉饰的暴力（"医院治疗"实为屠马场）

Boxer 到底是个怎样的人？
他不是 martyr（殉道者）——他没有选择牺牲。他是被欺骗和过度劳作杀害的受害者。他的忠诚被利用了，他的善良被滥用了。他的故事是全书对"盲目忠诚"最有力的控诉。
