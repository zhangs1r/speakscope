A few days later, when the terror caused by the executions had died down, some of the animals remembered — or thought they remembered — that the Sixth Commandment decreed "No animal shall kill any other animal." And though no one cared to mention it in the hearing of the pigs or the dogs, it was felt that the killings which had taken place did not square with this. Clover asked Benjamin to read her the Sixth Commandment, and when Benjamin, as usual, said that he refused to meddle in such matters, she fetched Muriel. Muriel read the Commandment for her. It ran: "No animal shall kill any other animal WITHOUT CAUSE." Somehow or other, the last two words had slipped out of the animals' memory. But they saw now that the Commandment had not been violated; for clearly there was good reason for killing the traitors who had leagued themselves with Snowball.
几天后，处决造成的恐怖逐渐平息，部分动物想起——或者以为自己想起——第六诫写的是"任何动物不得杀害其他动物"。虽然没人敢在猪或狗听得到的地方提起，但他们都觉得那些处决似乎与这条不符。Clover 请 Benjamin 念第六诫给她听，Benjamin 一如既往地说他不掺和这种事，于是她去找了 Muriel。Muriel 为她念了那条诫命。上面写着："任何动物不得无故杀害其他动物。"不知怎的，最后两个字被动物们遗忘了。不过现在他们明白了，这条诫命并没有被违反——因为处决那些与 Snowball 勾结的叛徒显然是有充分理由的。
⚠️ 这是全书最关键的篡改之一。 "WITHOUT CAUSE"（无故）两个字被加到了第六诫末尾。注意 Orwell 的用词：square with（与……相符）、meddle in（干涉）。Benjamin 拒绝掺和——他又一次选择沉默。而动物们"somehow or other"忘记了最后两个字——这正是 Orwell 对集体记忆被操控的精准刻画。
Throughout the year the animals worked even harder than they had worked in the previous year. To rebuild the windmill, with walls twice as thick as before, and to finish it by the appointed date, together with the regular work of the farm, was a tremendous labour. There were times when it seemed to the animals that they worked longer hours and fed no better than they had done in Jones's day. On Sunday mornings Squealer, holding down a long strip of paper with his trotter, would read out to them lists of figures proving that the production of every class of foodstuff had increased by two hundred per cent, three hundred per cent, or five hundred per cent, as the case might be. The animals saw no reason to disbelieve him, especially as they could no longer remember very clearly what conditions had been like before the Rebellion. All the same, there were days when they felt that they would sooner have had less figures and more food.
整个这一年，动物们比前一年工作得更加辛苦。重建风车——墙要比之前厚一倍，在指定日期前完工——加上农场日常劳作，是一项极其艰巨的苦役。有时候动物们觉得，他们干的时间比琼斯时代更长，吃得却并不比那时候好。每周日上午，Squealer 用蹄子按着一张长长的纸条，给他们念一串串数字，证明各种食物的产量分别增长了百分之两百、三百甚至五百。动物们没有理由不相信他——尤其是他们已经不太记得清楚革命前是什么样了。尽管如此，有些日子里他们觉得，宁愿少一点数字，多一点食物。
💡 Squealer 的宣传技巧：用看似精确的"数据"来证明一切都在变好。注意最后一句"less figures and more food"——Orwell 用这种朴素的语言道出了动物们最真实、最可悲的困境。他们不是被说服了，而是连反驳的记忆力都被剥夺了。
All orders were now issued through Squealer or one of the other pigs. Napoleon himself was not seen in public as often as once in a fortnight. When he did appear, he was attended not only by his retinue of dogs but by a black cockerel who marched in front of him and acted as a kind of trumpeter, letting out a loud "cock-a-doodle-doo" before Napoleon spoke. Even in the farmhouse, it was said, Napoleon inhabited separate apartments from the others. He took his meals alone, with two dogs to wait upon him, and always ate from the Crown Derby dinner service which had been in the glass cupboard in the drawing-room. It was also announced that the gun would be fired every year on Napoleon's birthday, as well as on the other two anniversaries.
现在所有命令都通过 Squealer 或其他猪来传达。Napoleon 本人每一两周才公开露面一次。当他出现时，不仅有一群狗随从，还有一只黑色小公鸡走在他前面充当号手——Napoleon 说话之前先来一声响亮的"咯咯咯——"。据说即使在农舍里，Napoleon 也住在与其他人分开的套间。他独自用餐，由两条狗伺候，餐具用的是客厅玻璃柜里的皇家德比瓷器。还宣布说，除了另外两个纪念日之外，每年 Napoleon 生日也要鸣枪。
💡 独裁者的仪式感。 黑公鸡当号手、狗当侍从、专用瓷器——Orwell 用这些细节描绘出 Napoleon 如何把自己一步步神话化。retinue（随从）本是王室用语。注意频率——"not seen as often as once in a fortnight"——领袖越神秘，权力越大。
Napoleon was now never spoken of simply as "Napoleon." He was always referred to in formal style as "our Leader, Comrade Napoleon," and the pigs liked to invent for him such titles as Father of All Animals, Terror of Mankind, Protector of the Sheep-fold, Ducklings' Friend, and the like. In his speeches, Squealer would talk with the tears rolling down his cheeks of Napoleon's wisdom the goodness of his heart, and the deep love he bore to all animals everywhere, even and especially the unhappy animals who still lived in ignorance and slavery on other farms. It had become usual to give Napoleon the credit for every successful achievement and every stroke of good fortune. You would often hear one hen remark to another, "Under the guidance of our Leader, Comrade Napoleon, I have laid five eggs in six days"; or two cows, enjoying a drink at the pool, would exclaim, "Thanks to the leadership of Comrade Napoleon, how excellent this water tastes!"
现在没人再直呼"Napoleon"了。正式场合他一律被称为"我们的领袖，Napoleon 同志"。猪们喜欢给他发明各种头衔：万兽之父、人类之恐、羊圈守护者、小鸭之友，诸如此类。Squealer 演讲时，眼泪会顺着脸颊滚落下来，歌颂 Napoleon 的智慧、他内心的善良、他对所有动物的深爱——尤其是那些仍然在其他农场的愚昧与奴役中生活的不幸动物。现在已经成了惯例，每一次成功、每一桩好运都归功于 Napoleon。你经常会听到一只母鸡对另一只说："在领袖 Napoleon 同志的指引下，我六天之内下了五个蛋"；或者两头牛在池塘边喝水时感叹："多亏了 Napoleon 同志的领导，这水喝起来真甜啊！"
🔥 个人崇拜的荒诞巅峰。 注意这些头衔的荒谬感——"Ducklings' Friend"（小鸭之友）与"Terror of Mankind"（人类之恐）放在一起。最讽刺的是动物们真的开始把生活的每一个细节都归功于 Napoleon——连母鸡下蛋和水的味道都要感恩领袖。这就是 Orwell 所说的"doublethink"的前奏。

Friend of fatherless!
Fountain of happiness!
Lord of the swill-bucket! Oh, how my soul is on
Fire when I gaze at thy
Calm and commanding eye,
Like the sun in the sky,
Comrade Napoleon!

Thou are the giver of
All that thy creatures love,
Full belly twice a day, clean straw to roll upon;
Every beast great or small
Sleeps at peace in his stall,
Thou watchest over all,
Comrade Napoleon!

Had I a sucking-pig,
Ere he had grown as big
Even as a pint bottle or as a rolling-pin,
He should have learned to be
Faithful and true to thee,
Yes, his first squeak should be
"Comrade Napoleon!"
无父者的朋友！
幸福的源泉！
泔水桶之主！哦，我的灵魂在燃烧——
当我凝视你
沉着而威严的眼睛，
如同天上的太阳，
Napoleon 同志！

你是万物所爱的
一切之赐予者，
一日两餐饱肚，干净的稻草可打滚；
大大小小的兽
在栏中安然入睡，
你守护着一切，
Napoleon 同志！

倘若我有一头乳猪，
在他长到
甚至只有一品脱瓶或擀面杖那么大之前，
他应该学会
对你忠诚和真实，
是的，他的第一声哼哼应该是——
"Napoleon 同志！"
📝 从革命颂歌到个人崇拜。 对比《Beasts of England》（动物们的革命战歌）和这首《Comrade Napoleon》（独裁者的颂歌）。前者是集体理想，后者是个人迷信。注意"swill-bucket"（泔水桶）——猪的意象贯穿始终。最后一段最讽刺：小猪还未断奶就要学会喊"Comrade Napoleon"——这就是 indoctrination（思想灌输）。
Napoleon approved of this poem and caused it to be inscribed on the wall of the big barn, at the opposite end from the Seven Commandments. It was surmounted by a portrait of Napoleon, in profile, executed by Squealer in white paint.
Napoleon 批准了这首诗，命人把它写在大谷仓的墙上——与七诫相对的另一端。上方还有一幅 Napoleon 的侧面肖像，由 Squealer 用白漆绘制。
💡 空间的象征。 颂诗在七诫对面——个人崇拜与"法律"相对而立。再过几章，七诫就会被完全取代。surmounted by（上方饰有）——这个词通常用于艺术品/雕塑的顶部装饰。
Meanwhile, through the agency of Whymper, Napoleon was engaged in complicated negotiations with Frederick and Pilkington. The pile of timber was still unsold. Of the two, Frederick was the more anxious to get hold of it, but he would not offer a reasonable price. At the same time there were renewed rumours that Frederick and his men were plotting to attack Animal Farm and to destroy the windmill, the building of which had aroused furious jealousy in him. Snowball was known to be still skulking on Pinchfield Farm. In the middle of the summer the animals were alarmed to hear that three hens had come forward and confessed that, inspired by Snowball, they had entered into a plot to murder Napoleon. They were executed immediately, and fresh precautions for Napoleon's safety were taken. Four dogs guarded his bed at night, one at each corner, and a young pig named Pinkeye was given the task of tasting all his food before he ate it, lest it should be poisoned.
与此同时，通过 Whymper 的中介，Napoleon 正与 Frederick 和 Pilkington 进行复杂的谈判。那堆木材仍然没有卖出去。两人中 Frederick 更急于得到它，但他不肯出合理的价格。与此同时，有传言说 Frederick 正和他的手下密谋进攻动物农场、摧毁风车——风车的建造激起了他极度的嫉妒。Snowball 据说仍然潜藏在 Pinchfield 农场。仲夏时节，动物们惊恐地听说有三只母鸡主动承认，受 Snowball 的指使策划谋害 Napoleon。她们被立即处决，并采取了新的安保措施：四条狗在夜间守护 Napoleon 的床，床的四角各一条；一只叫 Pinkeye 的小猪负责在他进食之前先尝食物，以防有毒。
💡 Whymper——人类律师，负责农场与外部世界的商业往来。skulking（潜藏）——Snowball 被塑造成一个躲在暗处的阴谋家。三只母鸡的"认罪"再次展示了认罪大会的模式。注意：lest（以防）——正式用语，后面的从句用虚拟语气。
At about the same time it was given out that Napoleon had arranged to sell the pile of timber to Mr. Pilkington; he was also going to enter into a regular agreement for the exchange of certain products between Animal Farm and Foxwood. The relations between Napoleon and Pilkington, though they were only conducted through Whymper, were now almost friendly. The animals distrusted Pilkington, as a human being, but greatly preferred him to Frederick, whom they both feared and hated. As the summer wore on, and the windmill neared completion, the rumours of an impending treacherous attack grew stronger and stronger.
大约同一时间，消息传出说 Napoleon 已安排将木材卖给 Pilkington 先生，还打算与 Foxwood 签订常规产品交换协议。Napoleon 与 Pilkington 的关系——虽然只通过 Whymper 进行——现在已近乎友好。动物们作为动物本能地不信任 Pilkington（因为他是人类），但他们还是宁愿要他也好过 Frederick——他们对 Frederick 既怕又恨。夏天慢慢过去，风车接近完工，关于一场背信弃义的进攻的传言变得越来越强烈。
Frederick, it was said, intended to bring against them twenty men all armed with guns, and he had already bribed the magistrates and police, so that if he could once get hold of the title-deeds of Animal Farm they would ask no questions. Moreover, terrible stories were leaking out from Pinchfield about the cruelties that Frederick practised upon his animals. He had flogged an old horse to death, he starved his cows, he had killed a dog by throwing it into the furnace, he amused himself in the evenings by making cocks fight with splinters of razor-blade tied to their spurs. The animals' blood boiled with rage when they heard of these things being done to their comrades, and sometimes they clamoured to be allowed to go out in a body and attack Pinchfield Farm, drive out the humans, and set the animals free. But Squealer counselled them to avoid rash actions and trust in Comrade Napoleon's strategy.
据说 Frederick 打算带二十个全副武装的人来进攻，而且他已经贿赂了地方法官和警察——这样一旦他拿到动物农场的产权证，他们就不会过问。此外，关于 Frederick 对自己农场动物的残酷行径的可怕传闻正从 Pinchfield 泄露出来。他鞭打一匹老马至死，让自己的牛挨饿，把一条狗扔进炉子里烧死，晚上还以让公鸡绑着剃刀片的距铁互相斗鸡为乐。动物们听到这些发生在自己同志身上的事，热血沸腾、怒火中烧，有时他们吵着要集体出动进攻 Pinchfield 农场，赶走人类，解放那里的动物。但 Squealer 劝他们避免鲁莽行动，相信 Napoleon 同志的战略。
⚠️ 注意 Orwell 的叙述技巧。 这些关于 Frederick 暴行的故事极有可能也是 propaganda（宣传）——为了塑造外敌形象、凝聚内部团结。注意 bribed the magistrates（贿赂法官）——暗示人类社会法律系统的腐败。flogged（鞭打）——强烈的暴力词汇。
Nevertheless, feeling against Frederick continued to run high. One Sunday morning Napoleon appeared in the barn and explained that he had never at any time contemplated selling the pile of timber to Frederick; he considered it beneath his dignity, he said, to have dealings with scoundrels of that description. The pigeons who were still sent out to spread tidings of the Rebellion were forbidden to set foot anywhere on Foxwood, and were also ordered to drop their former slogan of "Death to Humanity" in favour of "Death to Frederick." In the late summer yet another of Snowball's machinations was laid bare. The wheat crop was full of weeds, and it was discovered that on one of his nocturnal visits Snowball had mixed weed seeds with the seed corn.
尽管如此，对 Frederick 的敌意仍然高涨。一个星期天的早晨，Napoleon 出现在谷仓里，解释说他从没想过要把那批木材卖给 Frederick——他说，与那种无赖打交道有失身份。原本被派出去传播革命消息的鸽子现在被禁止在 Foxwood 落地，还被命令把口号从"人类去死"改成"Frederick 去死"。夏末，Snowball 的又一个阴谋被揭露了。麦田里长满了野草——据发现，Snowball 在一次夜间来访时将草籽混进了种子粮里。
🔥 经典的政治操作。 先塑造一个外敌（Frederick），把口号从"人类去死"改成特定人名——操控敌人的身份是宣传机器的核心功能。scoundrels（恶棍/无赖）——Napoleon 当众骂 Frederick 为 scoundrel，为后文的秘密交易做铺垫。machinations（阴谋诡计）——这个词带有强烈的戏剧性恶意。
In the autumn, by a tremendous, exhausting effort — for the harvest had to be gathered at almost the same time — the windmill was finished. The machinery had still to be installed, and Whymper was negotiating the purchase of it, but the structure was completed. In the teeth of every difficulty, in spite of inexperience, of primitive implements, of bad luck and of Snowball's treachery, the work had been finished punctually to the very day! Tired out but proud, the animals walked round and round their masterpiece, which appeared even more beautiful in their eyes than when it had been built the first time. Moreover, the walls were twice as thick as before. Nothing short of explosives would lay them low this time! Napoleon himself, attended by his dogs and his cockerel, came down to inspect the completed work; he personally congratulated the animals on their achievement, and announced that the mill would be named Napoleon Mill.
秋天，经过一番极其艰苦卓绝的努力——因为收割几乎要在同一时间进行——风车终于建成了。机械还没安装，Whymper 正在谈判采购，但主体结构已经完工。克服了一切困难——缺乏经验、原始工具、运气不佳、Snowball 的背叛——工程居然如期完成！又累又自豪的动物们绕着自己的杰作走了一圈又一圈，在他们眼中，它比第一次建造时还要美丽。而且墙比以前厚了一倍——这次除非用炸药，否则休想把它推倒！Napoleon 本人，在群狗和公鸡的簇拥下，下来视察了竣工的工程；他亲自祝贺动物们的成就，并宣布这座磨坊将被命名为Napoleon 磨坊。
💡 Napoleon Mill——命名权是权力的终极象征。风车是动物们用血汗建造的，但 Napoleon 把它以自己的名字命名。注意"Nothing short of explosives"这句话——这是讽刺性的 foreshadowing（伏笔），因为后面真的会用炸药。
Two days later the animals were called together for a special meeting in the barn. They were struck dumb with surprise when Napoleon announced that he had sold the pile of timber to Frederick. Tomorrow Frederick's wagons would arrive and begin carting it away. Throughout the whole period of his seeming friendship with Pilkington, Napoleon had really been in secret agreement with Frederick. All relations with Foxwood had been broken off; insulting messages had been sent to Pilkington. The pigeons had been told to avoid Pinchfield Farm and to alter their slogan from "Death to Frederick" to "Death to Pilkington."
两天后，动物们被召集到谷仓开特别会议。当 Napoleon 宣布他已将那批木材卖给了 Frederick 时，他们惊得目瞪口呆。明天 Frederick 的马车就要来把它拉走。在表面上与 Pilkington 友好的整个时期，Napoleon 实际上一直与 Frederick 私下有协议。与 Foxwood 的所有关系都已断绝；侮辱性的信息已经发给了 Pilkington。鸽子们被通知避开 Pinchfield 农场，并把口号从"Frederick 去死"改成"Pilkington 去死"。
⚠️ 话术操控的教科书。 口号从"Death to Humanity"→"Death to Frederick"→"Death to Pilkington"。敌人是谁不重要——重要的是始终有一个敌人。Napoleon 当着动物面骂 Frederick 是 scoundrel，背地里却和他做交易。动物们目瞪口呆——但他们会做什么吗？
The pigs were in ecstasies over Napoleon's cunning. By seeming to be friendly with Pilkington he had forced Frederick to raise his price by twelve pounds. But the superior quality of Napoleon's mind, said Squealer, was shown in the fact that he trusted nobody, not even Frederick. Frederick had wanted to pay for the timber with something called a cheque, which, it seemed, was a piece of paper with a promise to pay written upon it. But Napoleon was too clever for him. He had demanded payment in real five-pound notes, which were to be handed over before the timber was removed. Already Frederick had paid up; and the sum he had paid was just enough to buy the machinery for the windmill.
猪们对 Napoleon 的精明计谋欣喜若狂。通过假装与 Pilkington 友好，他迫使 Frederick 把价格提高了十二英镑。但 Squealer 说， Napoleon 思想的卓越之处体现在他不信任任何人——甚至包括 Frederick。Frederick 想用一张叫"支票"的东西来付木材款——那似乎是一张写着付款承诺的纸。但 Napoleon 太聪明了。他要求用真正的五英镑纸币支付，必须在木材运走之前交钱。Frederick 已经付清了；这笔钱正好够买风车的机器。
💡 ecstasies（狂喜）——猪们的反应与普通动物的无知形成对比。注意作者对"cheque"的解释——"a piece of paper with a promise to pay"——以动物的视角来解释人类金融概念，既幽默又暴露了他们的天真。
Meanwhile the timber was being carted away at high speed. When it was all gone, another special meeting was held in the barn for the animals to inspect Frederick's bank-notes. Smiling beatifically, and wearing both his decorations, Napoleon reposed on a bed of straw on the platform, with the money at his side, neatly piled on a china dish from the farmhouse kitchen. The animals filed slowly past, and each gazed his fill. And Boxer put out his nose to sniff at the bank-notes, and the flimsy white things stirred and rustled in his breath.
与此同时，木材被飞速运走。全部运完后，谷仓里又开了一次特别会议，让动物们检视 Frederick 的钞票。Napoleon 面带极乐的微笑，佩戴着他的两枚勋章，安坐在讲台上的稻草铺上，钱放在他身边，整齐地叠放在一个农场厨房的瓷盘里。动物们慢慢列队走过，每个人都看个够。Boxer 伸出鼻子去嗅那些钞票，那些薄薄的白色东西在他的鼻息中微微颤动、沙沙作响。
📸 极具画面感的场景。 Napoleon 像国王一样端坐，面前摆着一盘"钱"让臣民瞻仰。beatifically（极乐地）——这个宗教用语暗示 Napoleon 已经把自己摆在了神的位置。reposed（安坐）——充满王权意味。flimsy white things stirred and rustled——Boxer 的鼻息吹动了假钞！Orwell 这个细节太妙了：假钞连 Boxer 的鼻息都经不住。
Three days later there was a terrible hullabaloo. Whymper, his face deadly pale, came racing up the path on his bicycle, flung it down in the yard and rushed straight into the farmhouse. The next moment a choking roar of rage sounded from Napoleon's apartments. The news of what had happened sped round the farm like wildfire. The banknotes were forgeries! Frederick had got the timber for nothing!
三天后，一片可怕的喧闹爆发了。Whymper 脸色惨白，骑着自行车飞快地冲上小路，把车往院子里一扔，径直冲进了农舍。紧接着，从 Napoleon 的套房里传来一声被呛住了似的愤怒咆哮。消息像野火一样传遍了农场——那些钞票是假钞！Frederick 白白拿走了木材！
🔥 全章的高潮反转。 hullabaloo（喧闹/骚动）——这个拟声词非常生动。forgeries（伪造品/假钞）。Napoleon 自以为聪明——要了"真"钞票而不是支票——结果被更狡猾的 Frederick 骗了。"choking roar of rage"——一个独裁者被骗后的暴怒形象跃然纸上。
Napoleon called the animals together immediately and in a terrible voice pronounced the death sentence upon Frederick. When captured, he said, Frederick should be boiled alive. At the same time he warned them that after this treacherous deed the worst was to be expected. Frederick and his men might make their long-expected attack at any moment. Sentinels were placed at all the approaches to the farm. In addition, four pigeons were sent to Foxwood with a conciliatory message, which it was hoped might re-establish good relations with Pilkington.
Napoleon 立即召集动物们，用可怕的声音对 Frederick 宣判了死刑。他说，抓到 Frederick 后要把他活活煮死。同时他警告说，这次背信弃义的行为之后，最坏的情况可能随时发生。Frederick 和他的人可能随时发动人们期待已久的进攻。在农场的所有入口都布置了哨兵。此外，四只鸽子被派往 Foxwood，带去和解的信息——希望能与 Pilkington 重修旧好。
💡 conciliatory（和解的/安抚的）——刚刚把 Pilkington 当敌人咒骂，现在又派鸽子去求和。外交手腕完全没有原则，只有利益。注意"boiled alive"——Napoleon 的愤怒充满了暴力色彩。
The very next morning the attack came. The animals were at breakfast when the look-outs came racing in with the news that Frederick and his followers had already come through the five-barred gate. Boldly enough the animals sallied forth to meet them, but this time they did not have the easy victory that they had had in the Battle of the Cowshed. There were fifteen men, with half a dozen guns between them, and they opened fire as soon as they got within fifty yards. The animals could not face the terrible explosions and the stinging pellets, and in spite of the efforts of Napoleon and Boxer to rally them, they were soon driven back. A number of them were already wounded. They took refuge in the farm buildings and peeped cautiously out from chinks and knot-holes.
第二天一早，进攻就来了。动物们正在吃早餐，哨兵冲进来报告说 Frederick 和他的随从已经穿过了五栅门。动物们颇为勇敢地出发迎敌，但这次他们不像牛棚之战那样轻易取胜了。对方有十五个人、六支枪，一进入五十码以内就开始射击。动物们无法面对可怕的爆炸和刺人的霰弹——尽管 Napoleon 和 Boxer 竭力重整队伍——很快就被击退了。不少动物已经受伤。他们躲进农舍，从缝隙和木节孔中小心翼翼地往外窥视。
💡 sallied forth（出击/冲锋）——文学用语，带有英雄色彩。knot-holes（木节孔/树节洞）——这个视角转换太精妙了：动物们从木节孔里往外看，用动物的视角呈现战争的恐怖。
The whole of the big pasture, including the windmill, was in the hands of the enemy. For the moment even Napoleon seemed at a loss. He paced up and down without a word, his tail rigid and twitching. Wistful glances were sent in the direction of Foxwood. If Pilkington and his men would help them, the day might yet be won. But at this moment the four pigeons, who had been sent out on the day before, returned, one of them bearing a scrap of paper from Pilkington. On it was pencilled the words: "Serves you right."
整个大牧场——包括风车——都落在了敌人手里。此时连 Napoleon 都似乎不知所措。他来回踱步，一言不发，尾巴僵硬地抽动着。大家向 Foxwood 的方向投去渴求的目光——如果 Pilkington 能来帮忙，也许还能赢。但就在这时，前一天派出去的四只鸽子回来了，其中一只带回了一张 Pilkington 的纸条，上面用铅笔写着："活该。"
😬 "Serves you right"——短短三个单词，却是 Orwell 最冷酷的笔触之一。 Pilkington 完全看穿了 Napoleon 的投机把戏，冷漠地拒绝了援助。这是外交现实主义的残酷写照：没有永恒的盟友，只有永恒的利益。
Meanwhile Frederick and his men had halted about the windmill. The animals watched them, and a murmur of dismay went round. Two of the men had produced a crowbar and a sledge hammer. They were going to knock the windmill down.
与此同时，Frederick 和他的人在风车周围停了下来。动物们看着他们，一阵沮丧的低语传开。两个人拿出了一根撬棍和一把大锤——他们要拆掉风车。
"Impossible!" cried Napoleon. "We have built the walls far too thick for that. They could not knock it down in a week. Courage, comrades!"
"不可能！"Napoleon 喊道，"我们把墙造得那么厚，他们一周也敲不倒。鼓起勇气，同志们！"
But Benjamin was watching the movements of the men intently. The two with the hammer and the crowbar were drilling a hole near the base of the windmill. Slowly, and with an air almost of amusement, Benjamin nodded his long muzzle.
但 Benjamin 在专心地注视着那些人的动作。那两个拿锤子和撬棍的人在风车底部附近钻洞。Benjamin 慢慢地、带着一种近乎好笑的神情点了点他的长鼻子。
"I thought so," he said. "Do you not see what they are doing? In another moment they are going to pack blasting powder into that hole."
"我早料到了，"他说，"你们没看到他们在做什么吗？再过一会儿他们就要往那个洞里填炸药粉了。"
Terrified, the animals waited. It was impossible now to venture out of the shelter of the buildings. After a few minutes the men were seen to be running in all directions. Then there was a deafening roar. The pigeons swirled into the air, and all the animals, except Napoleon, flung themselves flat on their bellies and hid their faces. When they got up again, a huge cloud of black smoke was hanging where the windmill had been. Slowly the breeze drifted it away. The windmill had ceased to exist!
动物们恐惧地等待着。现在根本不可能冒险走出建筑物的掩护。几分钟后，只见那些人向四面八方奔逃。接着是一声震耳欲聋的巨响。鸽子被惊得飞上天空，所有动物——除了 Napoleon——都趴倒在地，把脸藏起来。当他们重新站起来时，风车所在的位置只剩下一团巨大的黑烟。微风慢慢把它吹散。风车已经不复存在了！
💥 全书最震撼的毁灭场景之一。 "ceased to exist"——三个简单的词，却承载了动物们一整年的血汗。注意：只有 Napoleon 没有趴下——"all the animals, except Napoleon"——这个细节意味深长：他是唯一不恐惧的人？还是他早就知道会这样？
