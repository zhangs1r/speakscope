All that year the animals worked like slaves. But they were happy in their work; they grudged no effort or sacrifice, well aware that everything that they did was for the benefit of themselves and those of their kind who would come after them, and not for a pack of idle, thieving human beings.
整整那一年，动物们像奴隶一样劳动。但它们干活是快乐的；它们不吝惜任何力气和牺牲，心里清楚自己做的每一件事都是为了自己——以及未来它们的后代——而不是为了那群懒惰、偷窃的人类。
💡 grudged（吝惜、不满）。开篇"worked like slaves"与"happy in their work"形成了刺痛的反讽。动物们以为自己不是奴隶，因为劳动"为了自己"——但付出和奴隶一样的劳动量。 这暗示了革命后的一个核心悖论：改变的只是"为谁而干"，而不是"怎么干"。
Throughout the spring and summer they worked a sixty-hour week, and in August Napoleon announced that there would be work on Sunday afternoons as well. This work was strictly voluntary, but any animal who absented himself from it would have his rations reduced by half. Even so, it was found necessary to leave certain tasks undone. The harvest was a little less successful than in the previous year, and two fields which should have been sown with roots in the early summer were not sown because the ploughing had not been completed early enough. It was possible to foresee that the coming winter would be a hard one.
整个春夏，他们每周工作六十小时。八月，Napoleon 宣布周日下午也要干活。这份工作是严格"自愿"的——但任何不参加的人口粮减半。即便如此，还是有一些任务不得不搁置。收成比去年略差，还有两块本应在初夏种上根茎作物的地没有播种——因为翻耕没有及时完成。可以预见，即将到来的冬天会很难熬。
💡 voluntary（自愿的）、rations（口粮）。"Strictly voluntary, but..."——"自愿"一词在这里已经失去了全部意义。 如果拒绝的代价是饿肚子，那就不存在选择。这是一个经典的政治修辞把戏：保留"自愿"的名义，抽走"自愿"的实质。六十小时工作周再加上周日加班——比琼斯时代更辛苦。
The windmill presented unexpected difficulties. There was a good quarry of limestone on the farm, and plenty of sand and cement had been found in one of the outhouses, so that all the materials for building were at hand. But the problem the animals could not at first solve was how to break up the stone into pieces of suitable size. There seemed no way of doing this except with picks and crowbars, which no animal could use, because no animal could stand on his hind legs. Only after weeks of vain effort did the right idea occur to somebody—namely, to utilise the force of gravity.
风车的建设遇到了意想不到的困难。农场上有一个很好的石灰岩采石场，还在一个外屋找到了大量沙子和水泥——建筑材料不愁。但动物们最初无法解决的问题是：如何把石头打碎成合适的大小。除了用镐和撬棍——动物们不能直立，所以用不了——似乎别无他法。经过数周徒劳的努力后，有人终于想到：利用重力。
💡 outhouses（外屋/附属建筑）、crowbars（撬棍）、utilise（利用）。动物们不会直立——这个生理限制成了全书最重要的隐喻之一。暴政的物理基础：当你不能直立时，你就无法使用工具，你就必须依赖那些能直立的人。
Huge boulders, far too big to be used as they were, were lying all over the bed of the quarry. The animals lashed ropes round these, and then all together, cows, horses, sheep, any animal that could lay hold of the rope—even the pigs sometimes joined in at critical moments—they dragged them with desperate slowness up the slope to the top of the quarry, where they were toppled over the edge, to shatter to pieces below. Transporting the stone when it was once broken was comparatively simple. The horses carried it off in cart-loads, the sheep dragged single blocks, even Muriel and Benjamin yoked themselves into an old governess-cart and did their share. By late summer a sufficient store of stone had accumulated, and then the building began, under the superintendence of the pigs.
巨大的石块——太大无法直接使用——散布在采石场底部。动物们用绳子捆住它们，然后所有动物一起——奶牛、马、羊，任何能抓住绳子的动物——连猪有时也在关键时刻加入——以令人绝望的缓慢速度把它们拖上坡顶，再从边缘推下去，让它们在下坠中摔碎。石头破碎后，运输相对简单：马儿整车装运，羊拖单块石头，连 Muriel 和 Benjamin 都把自己套在一辆旧的家庭马车里出一份力。夏末时，储存的石料足够多了——然后在猪的监督下，建设工作开始了。
💡 superintendence（监督）。这个画面既有史诗感又充满悲壮——所有动物齐心协力。注意"甚至猪有时也加入"——暗示猪已经越来越少参与体力劳动了。他们已经从劳动者变成了"监督者"。
But it was a slow, laborious process. Frequently it took a whole day of exhausting effort to drag a single boulder to the top of the quarry, and sometimes when it was pushed over the edge it failed to break. Nothing could have been achieved without Boxer, whose strength seemed equal to that of all the rest of the animals put together. When the boulder began to slip and the animals cried out in despair at finding themselves dragged down the hill, it was always Boxer who strained himself against the rope and brought the boulder to a stop. To see him toiling up the slope inch by inch, his breath coming fast, the tips of his hoofs clawing at the ground, and his great sides matted with sweat, filled everyone with admiration.
但这是一个缓慢而费力的过程。常常花整整一天精疲力尽地拖一块巨石到采石场顶部——有时推下去还摔不碎。如果没有 Boxer，什么都无法完成——他的力气似乎等于其他所有动物加在一起。当巨石开始下滑、动物们在绝望中尖叫着被拖下山坡时，总是 Boxer 拼命顶住绳子，让巨石停下来。看着他一寸一寸地爬坡，呼吸急促，蹄尖扒着地面，巨大的身躯上全是汗水——所有人都充满了敬佩。
💡 Boxer 的形象在这里达到近乎神话的高度——"strength seemed equal to that of all the rest of the animals put together"。但注意细节："his breath coming fast"和"matted with sweat"——这些不是英雄的勋章，而是身体被透支的警报。 Orwell 在铺垫：Boxer 正在被消耗殆尽。
Clover warned him sometimes to be careful not to overstrain himself, but Boxer would never listen to her. His two slogans, "I will work harder" and "Napoleon is always right," seemed to him a sufficient answer to all problems. He had made arrangements with the cockerel to call him three-quarters of an hour earlier in the mornings instead of half an hour. And in his spare moments, of which there were not many nowadays, he would go alone to the quarry, collect a load of broken stone, and drag it down to the site of the windmill unassisted.
Clover 有时提醒他小心别累坏了自己，但 Boxer 从不听她的。他的两句口号——"我要更加努力工作"和"拿破仑永远是对的"——在他看来足以回答一切问题。他已经和公鸡说好，每天早晨提前 45 分钟叫他，而不是原来的 30 分钟。在空闲时间——现在也不多了——他会独自去采石场，收集一车碎石，自己拖到风车工地。
💡 overstrain（过度劳累）。Boxer 把闹钟从提前半小时改成了提前四十五分钟——这个细节比任何大道理都更有力量。他用自己的命在换风车。 "I will work harder" + "Napoleon is always right" = 完美的被剥削者：又拼命又听话。Clover 的担忧代表了良知的声音，但良知在狂热面前总被忽视。
The animals were not badly off throughout that summer, in spite of the hardness of their work. If they had no more food than they had had in Jones's day, at least they did not have less. The advantage of only having to feed themselves, and not having to support five extravagant human beings as well, was so great that it would have taken a lot of failures to outweigh it. And in many ways the animal method of doing things was more efficient and saved labour. Such jobs as weeding, for instance, could be done with a thoroughness impossible to human beings. And again, since no animal now stole, it was unnecessary to fence off pasture from arable land, which saved a lot of labour on the upkeep of hedges and gates.
动物们整个夏天过得不算太糟——尽管工作很艰苦。就算它们不比琼斯时代吃得多，至少也没吃得更少。只需要养活自己、不需要再养活五个奢侈的人类，这个优势如此巨大——要很多次失败才能抵消。而且在很多方面，动物的做事方式更高效、更省力。比如除草这件事，动物们可以做得比人类彻底得多。再加上没有动物偷东西了，不再需要用篱笆把牧草地和耕地隔开——维护篱笆和大门的劳动力也省下了。
💡 thoroughness（彻底）。Orwell 在这里给了一个微妙的平衡——革命并非完全没有好处。"至少没有更糟"——这是一个非常低的底线。 但这种"知足"正是权力能够不断扩张的温床：因为比过去好一点，所以容忍现在的不好。
Nevertheless, as the summer wore on, various unforeseen shortages began to make themselves felt. There was need of paraffin oil, nails, string, dog biscuits, and iron for the horses' shoes, none of which could be produced on the farm. Later there would also be need for seeds and artificial manures, besides various tools and, finally, the machinery for the windmill. How these were to be procured, no one was able to imagine.
然而，随着夏天的流逝，各种未预料到的短缺开始显现。需要煤油、钉子、绳子、狗粮饼干和马掌铁——这些东西农场上都产不了。之后还需要种子、人工肥料、各种工具——最终还有风车的机器。这些东西怎么弄来，没人能想象。
💡 shortages（短缺）、paraffin oil（煤油）。注意这个列表：钉子、绳子、狗粮——这些看似不起眼的物品暴露了革命农业的根本矛盾：一个自给自足的动物乌托邦在工业社会里是不可能生存的。 你在乡下种地，但你仍然需要城市的煤油和铁器。
One Sunday morning, when the animals assembled to receive their orders, Napoleon announced that he had decided upon a new policy. From now onwards Animal Farm would engage in trade with the neighbouring farms: not, of course, for any commercial purpose, but simply in order to obtain certain materials which were urgently necessary. The needs of the windmill must override everything else, he said. He was therefore making arrangements to sell a stack of hay and part of the current year's wheat crop, and later on, if more money were needed, it would have to be made up by the sale of eggs, for which there was always a market in Willingdon. The hens, said Napoleon, should welcome this sacrifice as their own special contribution towards the building of the windmill.
一个周日早上，动物们集合接受命令时，Napoleon 宣布了一项新政策：从现在起，Animal Farm 将与邻近的农场进行贸易——当然，不是出于任何商业目的，只是为了获得某些急需的物资。风车的需要压倒一切，他说。因此他正在安排出售一堆干草和一部分今年的小麦收成；以后如果还需要更多钱，就卖鸡蛋——Willingdon 的鸡蛋市场一直很好。Napoleon 说，母鸡们应该欢迎这个牺牲——作为它们对建风车的特殊贡献。
💡 历史的讽刺时刻！革命的基本原则之一被推翻了：与人类进行贸易。 "Not for any commercial purpose"——这是标准的新话（doublethink）：明明就是商业行为，但你说它不是，它就"不是"。"The hens should welcome this sacrifice"——最精彩的荒诞：当权者替别人决定什么是"该欢迎的牺牲"。
Once again the animals were conscious of a vague uneasiness. Never to have any dealings with human beings, never to engage in trade, never to make use of money—had not these been among the earliest resolutions passed at that first triumphant Meeting after Jones was expelled? All the animals remembered passing such resolutions: or at least they thought that they remembered it. The four young pigs who had protested when Napoleon abolished the Meetings raised their voices timidly, but they were promptly silenced by a tremendous growling from the dogs. Then, as usual, the sheep broke into "Four legs good, two legs bad!" and the momentary awkwardness was smoothed over.
动物们再次感到一种模糊的不安。绝对不与人类打交道、绝不进行贸易、绝不用钱——这不正是琼斯被驱逐后的第一次胜利大会上通过的最早的决议吗？所有动物都记得通过过这样的决议——或者至少，它们以为自己记得。曾经在 Napoleon 取消大会时抗议过的四只小猪怯生生地发出了声音——但立刻被狗的巨大咆哮声压制住了。然后，照例，羊群爆发出"四条腿好，两条腿坏！"——瞬间的尴尬被抹平了。
💡 关键短语："or at least they thought that they remembered"——记忆已经开始变得不可靠。当历史不能被记录、不能自由讨论时，记忆就开始模糊。四只小猪再次试图抗议——但狗和羊的"合唱"配合得越来越默契：暴力压制异议，口号淹没噪音。
Finally Napoleon raised his trotter for silence and announced that he had already made all the arrangements. There would be no need for any of the animals to come in contact with human beings, which would clearly be most undesirable. He intended to take the whole burden upon his own shoulders. A Mr. Whymper, a solicitor living in Willingdon, had agreed to act as intermediary between Animal Farm and the outside world, and would visit the farm every Monday morning to receive his instructions. Napoleon ended his speech with his usual cry of "Long live Animal Farm!" and after the singing of 'Beasts of England' the animals were dismissed.
最后 Napoleon 抬起蹄子示意安静，宣布他已经做好了所有安排。动物们无需与人类接触——这显然是最不可取的。他打算由自己来承担全部负担。一位住在 Willingdon 的律师——Whymper 先生——已经同意作为 Animal Farm 和外界的中间人，每周一早上来农场接受指示。Napoleon 以他惯常的口号"Animal Farm 万岁！"结束了演讲——唱完《英格兰兽》后动物们解散了。
💡 trotter（猪蹄）、intermediary（中间人）。Napoleon 说"由我来承担全部负担"——听起来像是自我牺牲，实际上是把所有对外事务的控制权抓在手里。Whymper 的出场标志着 Animal Farm 正式进入"与敌人做交易"的阶段。 注意"And after the singing of 'Beasts of England'"——革命的圣歌仍在唱，但革命已经被掏空了。
Afterwards Squealer made a round of the farm and set the animals' minds at rest. He assured them that the resolution against engaging in trade and using money had never been passed, or even suggested. It was pure imagination, probably traceable in the beginning to lies circulated by Snowball. A few animals still felt faintly doubtful, but Squealer asked them shrewdly, "Are you certain that this is not something that you have dreamed, comrades? Have you any record of such a resolution? Is it written down anywhere?" And since it was certainly true that nothing of the kind existed in writing, the animals were satisfied that they had been mistaken.
之后 Squealer 在农场走了一圈，安抚大家。他向他们保证：那个反对贸易和用钱的决议从未被通过——甚至从未被提出过。这纯粹是想象，可能最初源于 Snowball 散布的谎言。少数动物仍然隐隐怀疑，但 Squealer 精明地问它们："你确定这不是你梦到的吗，同志们？你们有这样的书面记录吗？写在哪儿了？"由于确实没有任何书面记录，动物们便满意地接受了——它们记错了。
💡 这可能是全书最令人不寒而栗的段落。"Are you certain this is not something you have dreamed?" Squealer 的这个问题完美地利用了"没有文字记录"来否定现实。七诫没有被写下来吗？它们当然被写下来了——但谁去确认呢？当没有人能独立核实历史时，权力就可以重新定义现实。
Every Monday Mr. Whymper visited the farm as had been arranged. He was a sly-looking little man with side whiskers, a solicitor in a very small way of business, but sharp enough to have realised earlier than anyone else that Animal Farm would need a broker and that the commissions would be worth having. The animals watched his coming and going with a kind of dread, and avoided him as much as possible. Nevertheless, the sight of Napoleon, on all fours, delivering orders to Whymper, who stood on two legs, roused their pride and partly reconciled them to the new arrangement.
每周一 Whymper 先生按约定来农场。他是一个长相狡猾的小个子男人，留着连鬓胡子，是个生意很小的律师——但他足够精明，比别人更早意识到 Animal Farm 需要一个经纪人，而佣金是值得赚的。动物们带着一种恐惧看着他来来往往，尽量躲着他。然而，看到 Napoleon——四脚着地——向 Whymper（两腿站立）发号施令的场景，还是让它们感到骄傲，部分地接受了新安排。
💡 Whymper 是全书最有趣的次要角色之一。 他是一个投机分子——革命产生需求，他从中获利。"The sight of Napoleon delivering orders to a man who stood on two legs"——这个视觉画面让动物们感到虚幻的满足：看！我们的领袖在指挥人类！但这种满足掩盖了一个事实：他们正在与人类做交易——而这正是革命的初衷要禁止的。
🤔 你发现了吗？
第六章上半部分展示了革命在 经济压力下的妥协：

1. 劳动加重： "自愿"加班 + 60小时工作周——比琼斯时代更辛苦。

2. Boxer 的悲剧铺垫： 他越拼命劳动，越被体系榨取。"I will work harder"成了一道诅咒。

3. 短缺的必然性： 自给自足的乌托邦无法在工业社会中生存——钉子、煤油、铁器，这些东西都需要外界。

4. 记忆的篡改： "你确定你不是在做梦吗？"——当历史不能被可靠记录时，权力可以改写一切。

5. 中间人的出现： Napoleon 与 Whymper 的勾结——标志着 Animal Farm 正式进入与外部世界"正常化"的阶段。

⚠️ 每一次妥协都打着"风车需要"的旗号。"风车的需要压倒一切"——这个理由可以用来合理化一切背叛。
